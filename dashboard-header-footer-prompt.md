# Prompt: Dashboards that answer questions, a header that does work, and a footer credit

Paste everything below this line into the agent, from inside the repo root (`lucky-star-academy-sms/`). It assumes the UI redesign pass (`web/DESIGN.md`) has landed or is landing; build on those decisions, do not reopen them.

---

## THE JOB

Turn the three role dashboards in `web/src/app/{admin,teacher,student}/dashboard/page.tsx` from count tiles plus one bar chart into dashboards that answer the questions each user actually opens the app with, give the signed-in header in `src/components/layout/AppShell.tsx` real jobs — a calendar, notifications, and the contextual information a user needs at a glance — and add a footer credit across the product that reads exactly **Designed & Developed by AlbasaWEB**, linking to `https://albasaweb.com`.

Three specific corrections are part of this job. First, wherever the system counts or breaks down the people in the school it currently shows only Teachers and Students; Administrators must appear as a third group alongside them. That means an Administrators figure on the admin dashboard (`getDashboardStats` counts `profiles` where `role = 'teacher'` and can count `role = 'admin'` the same way), a people-breakdown visual that shows all three roles, and an **Administrators** entry in the admin sidebar's "People" section (`src/components/layout/nav.ts`) leading to a read-only list of administrator profiles built on the same `TableShell` pattern the Teachers page uses. Second, every chart must be relevant to the role viewing it — "students per class" is not the first thing a teacher needs to know on a Tuesday morning. Third, the header's decorative, unwired search box goes; nothing in the header may be ornamental.

Work in this order. Start by reading `web/PAGE-CONVENTIONS.md`, `web/DESIGN.md`, `src/lib/data/queries.ts` and `src/lib/supabase/database.types.ts`, so you know exactly which tables and columns exist: `attendance`, `exam_results`, `teacher_attendance`, `notices`, `complaints`, `students`, `subjects`, `classes`, `profiles`. Then write a short proposal, `web/DASHBOARDS.md`, listing for each role every visualization you intend to build as a row of four things: the question it answers, the chart form, the exact query or aggregation that feeds it, and why that form and not another. Include the header design (what the bell shows per role, what the calendar shows, what else sits in the bar) and the footer placement. Post the proposal and stop for a go-ahead before implementing. Then implement: read helpers first, then the shared chart components under `src/components/charts/`, then the header, then one dashboard at a time, then the footer, verifying each in the browser as all three roles at desktop and 360px.

For orientation, these are the kinds of questions the dashboards should answer, to be confirmed against the data you find. An administrator wants to know how attendance is trending across the school over recent weeks and which classes are below the others, how exam performance compares across classes and subjects and how marks are distributed rather than just averaged, whether every subject has a teacher and whether teachers were present today, how many complaints are open and how that has moved, and the make-up of the school by role and by class. A teacher wants to know which of their classes still has no attendance recorded today, how attendance in each of their classes has moved over recent weeks, which of their pupils are slipping below a threshold and need a word, and how marks in each subject they teach are spread. A pupil or parent wants to see attendance over time and by subject, marks by subject against the pass mark, and the newest notices — in a form a Primary 1–6 family can read on a phone without a legend.

## THE WHY

The current dashboards were ported feature-for-feature from a tutorial project: six counts and one bar chart that is the same chart for admin and teacher. Counts tell a head teacher how many pupils are enrolled, which she already knows; they do not tell her that Primary 4's attendance has fallen for three weeks, which is the thing the software exists to surface. The same is true of the header: a search field that searches nothing and no sense of what day it is or what needs attention makes the app feel like a demo. And the people who run the school are administrators too; a system that counts teachers and pupils but not the office is describing a school with nobody in charge. Finally, this is an ALBASA WEB deliverable and the credit line is how the next school hears about it — it has to be there, be correct, and be tasteful.

## THE GUARDRAILS

Every number and every chart must come from real rows in the database through Row Level Security. Do not invent sample data, placeholder series, fake notification counts, or calendar events that do not exist; when a series is empty, render a specific empty state that says what will fill it and how, in the voice `EmptyState` already uses. Do not fabricate a school calendar: there is no events table, so the calendar shows today's date, the days that carry real records (notices by their `date`, attendance days for this role), and navigates to the relevant page for a chosen day. If you believe a proper events or term calendar is worth having, propose the migration in `DASHBOARDS.md` as a follow-up; do not add tables, alter policies, or write migrations in this task.

You may add read helpers to `src/lib/data/*` following the existing pattern exactly — server-only, no `school_id` filter, no role check, RLS scopes the result — and you may extend `DashboardStats`. You may not touch `src/lib/actions/**`, `src/lib/auth/**`, `src/proxy.ts`, or anything under `supabase/**`. Aggregate on the server in the page or the helper and pass plain serialisable arrays to client chart components; never pass a function from a server component to a client component. Charts stay Recharts, stay `"use client"`, and receive an explicit `height`. Do not compute a comparison that RLS will not return — a pupil cannot see classmates' marks, so a "you versus class average" line needs a security-definer view or RPC and is therefore a proposal, not a hack around the policy.

Follow `DESIGN.md` and the theme: green is the working colour, gold is the accent, and chart series use a small ordered palette derived from them and defined once in one chart-tokens module, not per chart. Every chart has a title that states the question, axis labels with units, direct labels or a legend only when the form needs one, a text alternative (an `aria-label` or visually hidden summary) for screen readers, and colour that is never the only carrier of meaning. Prefer the plainest form that answers the question — a line for a trend, bars for comparison across categories, a small table when a table is clearer — and no pie charts with more than three slices, no 3D, no gauges, no animation on load. Dashboards need hierarchy: the one thing that most needs attention today sits first and largest; supporting figures sit smaller and lower; the six-identical-tiles grid is gone.

The header carries information, not chrome. The notifications bell shows a real, role-aware feed derived from existing tables — for admin, new complaints, subjects without a teacher, classes without attendance today, notices published this week; for teacher, own classes without attendance today and new notices; for student, new notices and newly recorded marks — with the badge hidden when the count is zero and each item linking to the page where it is dealt with. There is no read-state table, so define "new" by a fixed recent window and say so in the popover; do not fake a per-user unread state. The calendar is a popover, not a page. Show today's date in full, the school name, and the user's role in the bar; do not add a term or academic year unless that data exists somewhere. Remove the search field rather than leaving a decorative one. Keep the bar usable at 360px — icons collapse into an overflow before anything wraps.

The footer text is exactly `Designed & Developed by AlbasaWEB`, the link is `https://albasaweb.com` opening in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, styled small and muted in `text.secondary`, with the school name and the current year alongside. It appears on the landing page, the auth pages and the signed-in shell, sits below the content rather than fixed over it, and does not push the sign-in card off a phone screen. Do not add any other third-party link or badge.

Do not add dependencies beyond what is already installed. `npm run typecheck` must pass after every commit; commit in logical units (helpers, chart components, header, each dashboard, footer); do not run `npm run build` while the dev server is running. Update `PAGE-CONVENTIONS.md` to list any new read helpers and chart components. Everything currently on the dashboards that still earns its place stays; anything you remove is named in the final summary with the reason.

## DONE MEANS

The dashboards are done when a head teacher, a class teacher and a parent can each open their portal and, without clicking, learn something they would otherwise have had to ask someone: which class is slipping, which register is still blank, whether their child was in school this week. Concretely, all of the following are true.

`web/DASHBOARDS.md` exists, was approved before implementation, and matches what shipped. The admin dashboard shows Administrators alongside Teachers and Students in both its figures and its people breakdown, the admin sidebar has an Administrators entry under People, and that page lists administrator profiles read-only through `TableShell`. Each role's dashboard has at least three visualizations that are specific to that role and different from the other roles', led by the one that most needs attention, and no chart appears on two dashboards unless it answers a different question there. Every chart has a question-stating title, labelled axes, an accessible text alternative and an empty state; every figure traces to a query you can name; nothing is placeholder. Teachers can see at a glance which of today's registers are not yet taken; administrators can see attendance trending over weeks and compared across classes, exam performance compared across classes and subjects, and open complaints; pupils see attendance over time and by subject and marks against the pass mark, legibly at 360px.

The header shows today's date, the school, the user's role and name, a calendar popover marking real dated records and navigating to them, and a notifications bell fed by real rows with a badge that hides at zero and items that link to where they are resolved. The search box is gone. The footer credit reads exactly `Designed & Developed by AlbasaWEB`, links to `https://albasaweb.com` in a new tab, and appears on the landing page, the auth pages and the signed-in shell without overlapping content or breaking the mobile sign-in layout.

Every route renders for every role with no console errors or hydration warnings, `npm run typecheck` passes, nothing scrolls horizontally at 360px, an axe check reports no critical issues on the three dashboards, and `PAGE-CONVENTIONS.md` documents the new helpers and components. Before-and-after screenshots of each dashboard, the header with both popovers open, and the footer on the landing and login pages, at desktop and 360px, are saved to `Claude outputs/dashboards/`. The final message lists the visualizations built per role, the helpers added, anything removed and why, and any proposals — an events table, a class-average RPC — left for the owner to decide.
