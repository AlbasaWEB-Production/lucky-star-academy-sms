# Prompt: Expand the SMS into whole-school analytics — academic, people, finance, admissions, welfare

Paste everything below this line into the agent, from inside the repo root (`lucky-star-academy-sms/`). It runs after the dashboard, header and footer job (`web/DASHBOARDS.md`) has landed and assumes the analytics migrations it introduced — `public.terms`, `classes.campus`, `public.dashboard_thresholds`, the `v_*` views and `fn_at_risk_pupils()` — are in place. Build on them; do not recreate them.

---

## THE JOB

Extend Lucky Star Academy SMS from an attendance-and-marks system into a school analytics platform covering the domains a school actually runs on: academic performance, people and teaching operations, fees and finance, admissions and capacity, and pupil welfare. Where the data already exists, visualise it; where it does not, build the data model, the entry screens and the security policies first, and only then the charts. The reference brief below is the source of ideas, not a shopping list: it was written for large secondary schools and ERPs, and part of your job is to filter it for what it is — a Primary 1–6 school in Yendi with two campuses, Nayilifong and Kpatuya, run by a head teacher, a small office and a handful of teachers.

Work in phases and stop for a go-ahead at the end of every phase. Phase 0 is analysis only: read `MIGRATION.md`, `supabase/migrations/*`, `supabase/verify.sql`, `supabase/tests/rls_test.sql`, `web/PAGE-CONVENTIONS.md`, `web/DESIGN.md` and `web/DASHBOARDS.md`, then write `web/ANALYTICS-ROADMAP.md`. In it, take every visualization in the brief and place it in one of three columns — already possible from existing tables, needs new data (say exactly which table and columns), or not applicable to this school (say why) — and set out the phases below with the tables, policies, screens and charts each one delivers. Post it and wait.

Phase 1 is fees and finance, because it is the domain with the highest value and no data at all. Model a fee structure per class per term (amount, description, due date), an assessment generated per pupil from that structure, and payments against an assessment (date, amount, method — cash, mobile money, bank — and a receipt number generated by the system). Add an optional but recommended expenditure model: budget lines and actual spend by cost centre per term, so budget versus actual and a monthly cash position become possible. Build the admin screens to set fee structures, generate assessments for a class and term, record a payment and print or view a receipt, and to enter budget lines and expenses. Give pupils (and by extension their parents, who share the login) a view of their own balance, assessments and receipts only. Then chart it: fees collected against expected by term with a target line, outstanding by class as a composition, collection rate and average days to pay as KPI cards with a comparison to the previous term, a sortable defaulters table with balance and days overdue, cash position over months, and budget versus actual by cost centre with variance.

Phase 2 deepens academics using the term calendar that now exists. Give `exam_results` a `term_id` if it lacks one, give pupils an enrolment date and an enrolment status (active, withdrawn, completed, transferred) with a status date, and from those build enrolment trend over terms by class and campus, retention and dropout rates, pass and promotion rates against the pass mark in `dashboard_thresholds`, class average trend across terms, and a pupil progress card that shows one child's attendance percentage, marks by subject against the pass mark, fee balance and any welfare flags on one screen for the head and for that child's teachers.

Phase 3 covers people and teaching operations from data mostly present already: pupil–teacher ratio by class and campus, subject load per teacher (`v_teacher_subject_load`), teacher attendance rate over the term from `teacher_attendance`, and classes without a teacher for a subject. Professional-development hours and observation ratings are proposals in the roadmap, not builds, unless the head has asked for them.

Phase 4 is admissions and capacity: an admissions record with a stage (enquiry, application, offer, enrolled, declined) and dates, a seat capacity on each class, a funnel with conversion between stages, new enrolments by class and intake term, and capacity utilisation as enrolled over capacity per class, shown as a plain bar with a capacity line rather than a gauge.

Phase 5 is welfare: an incident record separate from complaints (date, pupil, class, type from a fixed list such as lateness, truancy, fighting, bullying, property damage, other; a short factual note; resolved flag and date), with incidents by type, incidents per hundred pupils by class, and the welfare flag on the pupil progress card. Co-curricular participation is a proposal unless the school records clubs.

Items the brief lists that you should record as not applicable and not build: GPA (marks and pass bands are the model here), LMS adoption and assignment submissions, device usage, bus routes and transport, classroom and lab utilisation heat maps, and radar charts of teacher evaluation scores.

Finish by reorganising the head teacher's dashboard into domains — Academic, People, Finance, Admissions, Welfare — each led by its single most important figure with its trend, expanding into the detail beneath, and refresh the teacher and pupil dashboards with the new data that concerns them (a teacher sees welfare incidents and fee status for their own classes only if the roadmap approves that; a pupil sees their own balance and receipts).

## THE WHY

Attendance and marks are done, and they were never the reason a school buys software. A primary school in Yendi runs on fee collection: whether the head can pay salaries at the end of the month depends on knowing today who has paid, which class is behind and how far. Parents want a receipt they can trust, and disputes about who paid what are the most common argument in a school office. Retention matters more in the Northern Region than in most places, and a child who stops appearing in the register is a story the system should tell before the term ends, not after. And the head cannot see the school as a whole when attendance lives on one page, marks on another and money in a ledger book. Building this properly, with `school_id` on every table and policies that hold, also turns a bespoke build into a product ALBASA WEB can offer the next school.

## THE GUARDRAILS

Security first, exactly as the existing migrations do it. Every new table carries `school_id`, has row level security enabled and gets policies per role in the style of `20260101000100_rls_policies.sql`; every new view declares `security_invoker = true`; every function is `security invoker` with `set search_path = ''`. For each new table add a check to `supabase/verify.sql` and cases to `supabase/tests/rls_test.sql` proving a teacher cannot read fee records, a pupil sees only their own assessments, payments and incidents, and nobody reads across schools. Migrations are new files numbered after `20260101000300`; never edit an applied migration, never drop or rename an existing column, and regenerate `src/lib/supabase/database.types.ts` with `npm run db:types` after every migration. Reference `public.terms` for every term-scoped record rather than storing term names as text, and read pass marks and at-risk rules from `dashboard_thresholds` rather than hard-coding them.

Money is stored in one way, documented in `MIGRATION.md` — pesewas as an integer or `numeric(12,2)`, choose one and use it everywhere — and formatted as Ghana cedis through a single formatter used by every screen and chart. Receipt numbers are generated in the database, sequential per school, and never reused; a payment is never deleted, only reversed by a compensating record with a reason, so the audit trail holds.

Do not add a new role in this task. Adding a bursar role touches `jwt_role`, `src/proxy.ts`, the auth actions and every policy; finance lives under the admin role for now, and a bursar role is a proposal in the roadmap with the exact files it would touch. Do not modify `src/lib/auth/**` or `src/proxy.ts`.

Respect the app's conventions. Server pages fetch through `src/lib/data/*` with no school filter and no role checks — RLS scopes the rows; writes go through server actions in `src/lib/actions/*` returning `FormActionResult`; never pass a function from a server to a client component; no MUI `Grid`; follow `DESIGN.md` and the chart tokens from the dashboards job; Recharts only; explicit heights; a question-stating title, labelled axes and a text alternative on every chart. Prefer plain forms — lines for trends, bars for comparison, stacked bars for composition, tables for lists — over gauges, treemaps, funnels drawn as trapezoids, 3D or animation; a funnel is a horizontal bar per stage with the conversion percentage written beside it. A KPI card shows a trend arrow only when a real prior period exists to compare against; otherwise it shows the figure alone. Sorting and filtering on tables is driven by `searchParams` read in the server page, not by client-side state, unless the roadmap argues otherwise for a specific table.

Protect the children. A pupil never sees another pupil's marks, attendance, balance or incidents; a teacher sees welfare and fee information only for classes they teach, and only if the roadmap approves that at all; the defaulters list and the incident register are admin-only. The incident note is short and factual and the form must not prompt for health, family or home circumstances. Never rank pupils against each other on any screen, and never display a class's defaulters in a place a pupil can reach. No invented data anywhere — no sample fees, seeded payments or demonstration incidents; every empty state says what will fill it and which screen does that.

Keep the working rhythm from the earlier jobs: `npm run typecheck` passes after every commit, commits are grouped per phase as migration, helpers and actions, screens, charts, no `npm run build` while the dev server runs, and `PAGE-CONVENTIONS.md` and `MIGRATION.md` are updated in the same phase that changes what they describe. Stop at every phase boundary and wait for a go-ahead; if the owner declines a phase, record that in the roadmap and continue to the next approved one.

## DONE MEANS

The head teacher's dashboard is organised into Academic, People, Finance, Admissions and Welfare, each led by one figure and its trend, and a head who opens it on a Monday can see without clicking how much of this term's fees are in, which class is furthest behind, whether enrolment is up or down on last term, which pupils are at risk and why, and whether anything happened last week that needs a conversation.

Finance works end to end: fee structures per class and term, assessments generated per pupil, payments recorded with system receipt numbers and reversals rather than deletions, a pupil or parent seeing their own balance and receipts and nothing else, and the admin charts — collected versus expected with a target line by term, outstanding by class, collection rate and days-to-pay with a previous-term comparison, a sortable defaulters table, monthly cash position, and budget versus actual by cost centre — all fed by real rows in Ghana cedis through one formatter. Academics show enrolment trend by class and campus over terms, retention and dropout, pass and promotion rates against the configured pass mark, class average trend, and a pupil progress card that brings attendance, marks, fees and welfare together for one child. People shows pupil–teacher ratio, subject load, teacher attendance rate and uncovered subjects. Admissions shows a stage funnel with conversions, new enrolments by class and intake, and capacity utilisation with a capacity line. Welfare shows incidents by type and per hundred pupils by class, and flags on the progress card.

Every new table has RLS with policies for all three roles; `verify.sql` returns zero rows for the missing-RLS and missing-`security_invoker` checks; `rls_test.sql` passes including the new cases that prove a teacher cannot read fees, a pupil sees only their own records and no row crosses a school; `database.types.ts` is regenerated; `npm run typecheck` passes; every route renders for every role with no console errors or hydration warnings; nothing scrolls horizontally at 360px; an axe check reports no critical issues on the head dashboard, the fees screens and the progress card. The items marked not applicable were not built. `PAGE-CONVENTIONS.md` and `MIGRATION.md` describe the new tables, helpers, actions and components. Before-and-after screenshots of the head dashboard, each new admin screen, the pupil balance view and the progress card, at desktop and 360px, are in `Claude outputs/analytics/`. The final message lists what each phase delivered, what was deferred or declined and why, and the proposals — the bursar role, professional-development tracking, co-curricular participation — left for the owner.
